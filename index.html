Sui Swap.

A Sui Swap trades one token for another directly on the Sui network, routed through an on-chain DEX such as Cetus or Turbos rather than through any party that holds your funds. Sui's object-centric Move model lets independent transactions execute in parallel instead of queuing one after another, so a swap confirms with sub-second finality even while the network is busy. You review the quote, the price impact and the slippage tolerance before you sign, pay a small fee in SUI, and the output token lands directly in your own wallet.

What Is a Sui Swap?

A Sui Swap is an on-chain trade of one token for another, executed by a decentralized exchange running on the Sui network instead of by a custodial order book. You connect a self-custody wallet, pick the pair you want, and the DEX's smart contract pulls the amount you are selling from a liquidity pool while sending the matching amount of the output token straight to your address. Nothing passes through a third party that could freeze or delay it. Because Sui processes unrelated transactions in parallel rather than forcing every trade through a single global queue, a swap that would compete for block space on a slower chain settles here in a fraction of a second, with the quoted price holding unless the pool moves before your transaction lands.

How a Sui Swap Executes on Cetus and Turbos

Most swap volume on the network runs through a small set of automated market makers, chiefly Cetus and Turbos Finance, each holding pooled liquidity for the pairs they list. You never trade against another person directly; you trade against the pool, and the pool's pricing curve sets the rate you get.

Why Sui's object model matters here

Sui treats assets as individually owned objects rather than entries in one shared account table, which is part of why the network can validate many swaps touching different pools at the same time instead of processing them one by one. The practical effect for a trader is a network that stays fast under load, confirming a route in well under a second even when many other swaps are happening at once.

Concentrated liquidity and routing

Cetus and similar Sui DEXes use concentrated liquidity, meaning providers can park their capital near the current price so a given pool size covers more volume with less slippage. When one pool does not have enough depth for your size, the router can split the trade across pools or hop through an intermediate asset, and the quote you are shown already reflects that path.

Sui Swap Fees, Gas, and Timing

A trade here carries two separate costs: the network's own gas fee, paid in SUI, and the DEX's swap fee, usually a small percentage set by the pool you trade through. Both are tiny compared with execution on a congested network, which is part of why routing a transfer through a chain like this is attractive for frequent trading. Why gas stays low on Sui

Sui's parallel execution means the network is not competing every transaction for the same serial slot, so fee pressure during busy periods stays modest compared with chains that process everything sequentially. You still pay gas in SUI for every swap, but it rarely becomes the deciding cost in a trade.

Judging a quote by the net output

Compare routes by the token amount you actually receive after fees and slippage, not the headline exchange rate. A pool with a lower listed fee can still net you less if its depth is thin and the price moves against you mid-trade, so check the minimum received figure the interface shows before you sign.

Running a Sui Swap Step by Step

The flow is the same on Cetus, Turbos or any other Sui DEX, which is what makes the process predictable once you have done it a few times.
  • Connect your self-custody wallet to the DEX and select the token you are selling and the token you want.
  • Enter the amount; the interface shows the quoted rate, the price impact and the minimum you will receive.
  • Set your slippage tolerance so the trade reverts rather than filling at a worse price.
  • Review the route, confirm the gas estimate in SUI, and sign the transaction in your wallet.
  • Watch for sub-second confirmation, then check the output token balance in your wallet.

Is a Sui Swap Safe to Run?

A trade through an established DEX never takes custody of your funds; the contract only moves what you approve for the single transaction you sign. The risks that remain are the ones you control at the moment of signing, not a counterparty holding your assets.

Slippage and price impact

A trade against a shallow pool can move the price against you before it fills, which is why the interface shows price impact separately from the fee. Setting a sensible slippage tolerance protects you from an unexpected fill; setting it too wide defeats that protection, and setting it too tight can cause the transaction to fail harmlessly when the market moves.

The one rule that never changes

No DEX, wallet or support channel ever needs your seed phrase to approve a trade. Anyone asking for it is attempting fraud. A genuine swap only ever asks you to sign a transaction you can read in your own wallet, listing the exact tokens and amounts involved.

Stablecoin Liquidity on Sui

Sui carries deep stablecoin pools, with USDC pairs among the most liquid routes on the network, which keeps a trade into or out of a dollar-denominated asset cheap and low-slippage even at meaningful size. That depth comes from both native issuance and bridged supply, tracked across pools the way any serious liquidity market is, and traders moving volume through Cetus or Turbos routinely check aggregated DeFi data before sizing a trade against it. Stablecoin-to-stablecoin routes tend to carry the lowest price impact of any pair on the network, since both sides hold close to the same market price. A trader researching the chain's design more broadly can start with the network's entry on Wikipedia before going deeper into the technical docs.

Swapping on Sui vs Other Chains

What sets trading on Sui apart from the same trade on an account-based chain is less the user interface and more what happens underneath it. Parallel execution from the object model means the network is not a single queue that every trade competes for, so fees and confirmation times stay stable instead of spiking under load. You can verify any settled trade directly on the Sui network explorer, and the underlying mechanics are documented in full in the Sui docs. For a DEX-specific view of pool mechanics and fee tiers, the Cetus documentation covers how concentrated liquidity on this network is structured, and the same explorer lets you confirm the output of your own transaction after it lands.
What is a swap on the Sui network?
It is an on-chain trade between two tokens, executed by a decentralized exchange such as Cetus or Turbos on Sui rather than by a custodial party. You keep your keys the entire time.
Which DEXes handle trading on Sui?
Cetus and Turbos Finance carry most of the liquidity on Sui today, each running automated market maker pools you trade against directly through your wallet.
Why does a trade settle so fast on Sui?
Sui's object-centric Move model lets independent transactions execute in parallel instead of queuing through one global sequence, so trades settle with sub-second finality even when the network is busy.
What does trading on Sui cost?
You pay a small network gas fee in SUI plus the pool's own swap fee, typically a fraction of a percent of the trade size. Both are usually negligible compared with the trade itself.
How do I avoid bad slippage when trading on Sui?
Set a slippage tolerance that matches the pool's depth, check the price impact shown before you sign, and size large trades against pools with enough liquidity, splitting across pools when needed.
Do I need an account to trade on Sui?
No. You connect a self-custody wallet and trade on-chain, with no sign-up and no custodian holding your funds at any point.
Can I trade stablecoins on Sui?
Yes. USDC and other stablecoin pairs are among the deepest pools on Sui, which keeps price impact low even for sizable trades between dollar-denominated assets.
